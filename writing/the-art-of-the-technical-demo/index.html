The art of the technical demo

A great demo is less about features and more about a buyer recognizing a better way to work.

QSQasim Shirazi
Shirazi AI Labs · Field notes

Start with the buyer

A feature checklist rarely communicates value on its own. Begin with the customer’s current workflow, the friction inside it, and what an improved outcome would look like.

Tell one story well

The most persuasive demonstrations have a beginning, middle, and end. Show a recognizable problem, make the transformation visible, and quantify the result where the evidence supports it.

Respect technical scrutiny

A technical buyer may care about integrations, data boundaries, security controls, and edge cases. Good demos anticipate those questions and separate what is live, simulated, or planned.

Close the loop

Leave the audience with a testable next step: a proof of value, an implementation outline, or a concise summary of agreed requirements. The purpose of a demo is understanding that supports a decision.
