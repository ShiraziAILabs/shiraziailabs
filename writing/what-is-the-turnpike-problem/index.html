Mathematics / Explainer

What is the turnpike problem?

Can we reconstruct points on a line if we know only the distances between pairs?

Distances without labels

Imagine several points on a straight road. You have the distances between every pair of points, but not the labels that reveal which points produced each distance. Could you recover the original arrangement?

That is the turnpike reconstruction problem, a classic example of inferring hidden structure from incomplete context.

Where ambiguity enters

Different configurations can sometimes fit the same distance information. Missing or repeated distances add further complexity. A careful investigation must account for these ambiguities rather than assume uniqueness.

Research direction

My work explores variants of reconstruction under missing observations, with an emphasis on clear assumptions and independently checkable mathematical arguments.
