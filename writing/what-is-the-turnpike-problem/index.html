What is the turnpike problem—and why does it matter?

How a deceptively simple distance puzzle leads to deep questions about reconstructing hidden structure.

QSQasim Shirazi
Shirazi AI Labs · Field notes

The puzzle

Imagine several towns arranged along a straight highway. You know every distance between pairs of towns—but not which town sits where. Can you reconstruct the original positions? This is the turnpike problem. The name comes from the interpretation of the positions as points along a road.

Why it gets interesting

The input is a multiset of distances, not a labeled map. Different pairs can have the same separation, and the order of the points is hidden. A reconstruction that looks plausible may not be the only one. Reflection and translation also preserve distances, so uniqueness has to be defined carefully.

The missing-information version

What happens if one distance measurement is omitted? A seemingly small loss of information can change the structure of the reconstruction task. Exploring these edge cases is one of the mathematical directions pursued at Shirazi AI Labs.

A broader lesson

Distance reconstruction is an example of an inverse problem: we see the consequences of an unknown structure and try to infer the structure itself. The same pattern appears in imaging, sensor localization, and scientific measurement.
